## 1. Lawsuit statistics 2023-2026 (volume, e-commerce share, states, platforms, size, repeat defendants, overlays)

### Takeaway
Digital accessibility litigation is at an all-time high and still growing: about 5,000 suits in 2025 (federal + NY/CA state courts) and UsableNet projects about 6,176 for 2026 (+20%). Roughly 70-79% target e-commerce, about two-thirds hit companies with under $50M revenue, about 46% of federal cases name repeat defendants, and around 1 in 5 defendants already ran an overlay widget. Exact totals differ by tracker because each counts different things.

### Inferences
- Even with the trackers disagreeing, the order of magnitude is clear: 3,000+ federal web suits a year plus 2,000-3,000 state suits, mostly NY and CA. The trend runs 2024 dip, 2025 rebound (+27% federal), 2026 still growing (+20% projected).
- The core target is small and mid-size e-commerce under $50M revenue, which is a good fit for the service. Shopify is the most-sued platform by volume, partly because it is the most common.
- "Install a widget" does not reduce risk, and over 20% of defendants had one. That is a direct sales argument for manual audit plus code remediation, but it must be phrased factually ("widgets did not prevent X% of suits") and not as a promise.
- The high repeat-defendant share (about 46% federal) shows that settling without real remediation leaves the store exposed. That is the business case for ongoing monitoring.
- Hot venues for prospecting are stores selling into NY, FL, IL and CA. The plaintiff's venue does not depend on where the store is based, so any store selling to US consumers is exposed.

## 2. Demand letters, settlements, defense costs, timelines, plaintiff firms

### Takeaway
Filed suits are only the visible tip: many more claims settle privately at the demand-letter stage, but no one publishes a verified count. Small-business settlements usually land at about $5k-$20k (sometimes under $5k), and total cost with defense can reach $15k-$90k for a filed case. Filings are highly concentrated: about 10-16 firms file 84-90% of cases.

### Cited Findings
- Seyfarth notes that defense lawyers handle many more privately settled demand letters than the cases that reach federal court. There is no count. [S] — [Level Access citing Seyfarth](https://levelaccess.com/blog/title-iii-lawsuits-10-big-companies-sued-over-website-accessibility/)
- Typical ADA website settlements run **$5,000-$20,000**, and small businesses often settle for **under $5,000**. Settling after a demand letter is cheaper than after a complaint or motions. [S, vendor] — [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/ada-website-compliance-lawsuit-settlement-amounts/)
- Businesses under $5M revenue often pay **$5,000-$10,000**. [S, vendor] — [WebAbility](https://www.webability.io/blog/ada-lawsuit-settlement-amounts)
- Opening demands for defendants under $10M are often **$25,000-$50,000**, and negotiated amounts often land at **50-70%** of the opening demand. One example: a $74,999 demand resolved for $2,000 plus about $10,000 in defense costs. A filed case for an SMB is estimated at **$15,000-$90,000 all-in**, and the settlement is rarely the biggest line. [S, vendor] — [TestParty demand-letter anatomy](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-demand-letter-anatomy-real-annotated); [TestParty lawsuit cost](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-website-lawsuit-cost)
- NFIB (small-business lobby) describes demand letters of up to **$15,000** in Missouri in a 2026 campaign document. [S] — [NFIB Missouri one-pager, Mar 2026](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Missouri-Issue-Campaign-One-Pager.pdf)
- California Unruh claims add **$4,000 minimum statutory damages per violation** plus attorney's fees, which raises settlement values there. Under the federal ADA alone, private plaintiffs get only injunctive relief and fees. [V: statute] — [Cal. Civ. Code §52 via multiple summaries](https://www.audioeye.com/compliance/california/); [BK: leginfo Civ. Code §52](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=52)
- **Plaintiff firms (2025):** **Equal Access Law Group** was the top filer with **641** suits (about 16% of 3,948, EcomBack). **16 firms filed 3,567 cases (90.35%)**. [S] — [TestParty citing EcomBack](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-lawsuit-trends-ecommerce-2025-2026-data). UsableNet 2026 H1: the **top 10 firms account for about 84%** of filings. [S] — [UsableNet midyear](https://info.usablenet.com/2026-midyear-report)
- **Gottlieb & Associates** (NY) reportedly filed 156 suits in Q1 2025. Manning Law, APC (CA) is also cited as a top filer. Rankings differ by source. Named serial plaintiffs tied to Gottlieb include Denise Crumwell, Frank Senior and Sandy Graciano. [S] — [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/sued-by-gottlieb-associates-for-ada-violations/); [TestParty on serial plaintiffs 2026](https://testparty.ai/blog/serial-ada-plaintiffs-2026); [Accessible.org list of 40 plaintiff firms](https://accessible.org/ada-website-plaintiffs-law-firms/)
- **Response timeline:** I found no verified source. Demand letters commonly set short deadlines (often cited as 14-30 days), and a federal complaint requires an answer within **21 days of service** under FRCP 12(a). [BK] — [FRCP Rule 12](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_12)

### Inferences
- The pain worth selling against is the total cost of one claim (settlement plus lawyer plus forced remediation), which is commonly $10k-$30k+ for a small store, and the risk of being sued again later. An audit package priced at $1.5k-$5k is small next to that.
- Because a few firms file the bulk of cases and reuse templated complaints, the issues they cite (missing alt text, unlabeled forms, keyboard traps, contrast, checkout/cart problems) are predictable. That is useful for scoping a "lawsuit-pattern" audit, but marketing must never say the audit guarantees no suit.

### Gaps
- No verified ratio of demand letters to filed suits. No neutral (non-vendor) data on settlement or defense-cost ranges.
- No confirmed 2025-26 activity data on Stein Saks, Mizrahi Kroub or Pacific Trial Attorneys (all historically active, per background knowledge).

## 3. Legal standards and court decisions

### Takeaway
There is still **no DOJ Title III web regulation** for private businesses in 2026. Liability rests on the ADA's general mandate, court interpretations (split on whether online-only businesses are covered), DOJ's non-binding March 2022 guidance, and state laws, mainly California's Unruh Act. WCAG 2.1 AA is the de-facto benchmark courts and settlements use, and WCAG 2.2 AA is the forward-looking target. The Title II rule for governments was delayed in April 2026 to April 2027 and April 2028. It is a separate, larger-ticket market.

### Cited Findings
- **No Title III web rule:** The ADA names no technical standard. Courts generally use WCAG 2.1 AA. Private businesses have no official equivalent of the Title II rule. [V] — [Level Access](https://levelaccess.com/blog/title-iii-lawsuits-10-big-companies-sued-over-website-accessibility/); [ABA Business Law Today, Aug 2025](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2025-august/digital-accessibility-under-title-iii-ada/)
- DOJ's earlier Title III web rulemaking was withdrawn on **26 Dec 2017** without a proposed rule. [V] — [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/doj-issues-web-accessibility-guidance.html)
- **DOJ guidance, 18 March 2022:** It states that Titles II and III cover websites of governments and of businesses open to the public. It does not have the force of regulation, and it points to WCAG and Section 508 as references while giving businesses flexibility. [V] — [US Access Board](https://www.access-board.gov/news/2022/03/21/department-of-justice-issues-web-accessibility-guidance-under-the-ada/); [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/insights/alerts/2022/us-doj-issues-guidance-on-web-accessibility-under-the-ada); [BK: ADA.gov guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)
- **Title II rule (state and local governments):** Final rule published April 2024, standard **WCAG 2.1 AA**. A DOJ **Interim Final Rule effective 20 April 2026** (91 FR 20902) extended compliance dates. Entities serving 50,000+ people move from 24 Apr 2026 to **26 Apr 2027**. Smaller entities and special districts move from 26 Apr 2027 to **26 Apr 2028**. The comment deadline was 22 June 2026. DOJ cites resource constraints and the limits of generative-AI remediation, signals further review of the requirements, and says the existing duty to be accessible continues. [V] — [Federal Register 2026-07663](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/20/2026-07663/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web); [SBA Office of Advocacy](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2026/04/27/doj-extends-compliance-dates-for-state-and-local-governments-to-make-their-websites-accessible/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/doj-extends-digital-accessibility-compliance-dates-under-title-ii-of-the-ada/); [Seyfarth ADA Title III blog, Apr 2026](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/04/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-website-accessibility-deadlines-for-governmental-entities-but-litigation-and-compliance-risks-remain/)
- **California Unruh Act:** $4,000 minimum statutory damages per violation plus fees, with no proof of monetary harm required. Under §51(f), any ADA violation is also an Unruh violation. A California appellate ruling (Martinez v. Cot'n Wash, 2022 [BK]) held that online-only businesses are not ADA "places of public accommodation", which pushed CA web claims into state court as Unruh intentional-discrimination claims. [V/S] — [AudioEye CA](https://www.audioeye.com/compliance/california/); [NK Legal](https://www.nklegal.com/post/california-website-accessibility-lawsuits-what-smbs-must-know)
- **CA reform:** **AB 649** (Small Business Right to Cure Program) passed the Assembly 78-0 but was **held on the Senate Appropriations suspense file on 13 Aug 2026**, so it did not pass in 2026. [S] — [remediada blog](https://www.remediada.com/blog/ada-website-lawsuits-2026); [Converge Accessibility Aug 2026 update](https://convergeaccessibility.com/2026/08/31/legal-update-august-2026/). I could not confirm any SB 84 Unruh reform.
- **Federal reform:** **HR 7328** would add pre-suit administrative steps before small-business website suits. It has sat in House Energy & Commerce since 3 Feb 2026 with no movement. [S] — [remediada](https://www.remediada.com/blog/ada-website-lawsuits-2026)
- **New York:** No NY-specific web statute or notice-and-cure law was found for 2026. Claims use the ADA plus the NY State and NYC Human Rights Laws. Several SDNY judges have held that standalone websites are not public accommodations, while others in the same court disagree, and stricter standing rules have pushed filings toward state court. [S] — [remediada](https://www.remediada.com/blog/ada-website-lawsuits-2026); [UsableNet blog](https://blog.usablenet.com/inside-the-2026-midyear-numbers-where-digital-accessibility-litigation-is-going)
- **Key cases (background):** *Robles v. Domino's* (9th Cir. 2019) held that the website and app had to be accessible because of their nexus to physical stores. **The Supreme Court denied cert on 7 Oct 2019** (No. 18-1539). [BK; the cert petition is confirmed in results] — [Supreme Court docket 18-1539](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/18/18-1539/113926/20190828122403646_DominosCertReply.pdf). *Gil v. Winn-Dixie* (11th Cir. 2021) held that a website is not a public accommodation, but the panel opinion was **vacated as moot in Dec 2021**, leaving the 11th Circuit without binding precedent. [BK] — [Seyfarth ADA Title III blog](https://www.adatitleiii.com/). Circuits are split between "nexus to a physical place" (9th, 11th, 3rd, 6th) and broader coverage (1st, 2nd, 7th tend broader in district courts). [BK]
- **WCAG version:** Courts, DOJ (in the Title II rule) and most settlements reference **WCAG 2.1 AA**. WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation in Oct 2023 [BK] and is used as a forward-looking target. [V for 2.1 AA as benchmark] — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/20/2026-07663/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web); [Level Access](https://levelaccess.com/blog/title-iii-lawsuits-10-big-companies-sued-over-website-accessibility/)

### Inferences
- The service should audit to **WCAG 2.2 AA**, which includes 2.1 AA, and report conformance against 2.1 AA as the litigated benchmark.
- The legal uncertainty (no rule, a circuit split) is why plaintiffs pick friendly venues and why defendants settle rather than litigate. That keeps demand steady.
- Title II is a separate market (government and public universities) with hard deadlines in 2027 and 2028, larger contracts and procurement rules. It is worth noting, but it does not overlap with small e-commerce.
- Reform risk to demand: if a right-to-cure law passes (CA AB 649 or federal HR 7328), urgency drops for stores that have not been sued, but post-notice remediation demand would rise. Neither had passed as of Oct 2026.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the 2022 DOJ guidance is still posted on ADA.gov after the 2025 administration's guidance rescissions.
- No 2025-26 appellate decisions on websites as public accommodations were retrieved.
- The text and status of NY 2025-26 bills, and the full set of 2026 CA bills, were not verified on official legislature sites.

## 4. Does remediation reduce lawsuit risk? (Statements, monitoring)

### Takeaway
No source shows that remediation eliminates risk, and there is no neutral study measuring risk reduction. Indirect evidence (repeat defendants, overlay sites being sued, settlements not barring new plaintiffs) shows that only real, ongoing conformance plus documentation lowers exposure and improves the defense posture.

### Cited Findings
- About 46% of federal 2025 cases named repeat defendants, and 1,427 suits targeted previously sued companies. [S] — [AccessNewswire/UsableNet](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/computers-technology-and-internet/digital-accessibility-lawsuits-surge-in-2025-1055151)
- A prior settlement or consent decree does not stop new plaintiffs from suing. [S] — [Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/can-you-be-sued-over-digital-accessibility-if-you-already-have-a-settlement-spoiler-alert-yes/)
- Overlays do not provide equivalent access. Courts (2021 case) and the FTC (accessiBe) have rejected "automatic compliance" claims, and 20%+ of sued sites ran overlays. [S/V] — [WebAbility](https://www.webability.io/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuit); [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million)
- Best-practice mitigation recommended by the defense bar includes a WCAG 2.1 AA audit by qualified testers including people with disabilities, a remediation plan, an accessibility statement with a feedback channel, training and periodic monitoring. [S] — [ABA Business Law Today, Aug 2025](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2025-august/digital-accessibility-under-title-iii-ada/)

### Inferences
- An honest pitch: remediation and monitoring **reduce the attack surface** (plaintiff firms' testers use screen readers and automated scans to find easy targets), **give the store documentation** to show its lawyer, and may support mootness or lower settlement leverage. It does **not** make a store "lawsuit-proof".
- An accessibility statement is good practice and a feedback channel, but it has no legal safe-harbor value on its own.

### Gaps
- No neutral quantitative study comparing lawsuit rates for remediated vs non-remediated stores.
- No verified data on whether a published accessibility statement changes plaintiff targeting.

## 5. Claim risks: FTC vs accessiBe, deceptive marketing, disclaimers, unauthorized practice of law

### Takeaway
The FTC's 2025 accessiBe order is the template for what not to say: no unsupported claims that a product or service makes or keeps a site WCAG- or ADA-compliant, and no fake "independent" reviews. A vendor must use "not legal advice" disclaimers, must not negotiate or advise on demand letters (that is the practice of law), and should partner with US defense attorneys.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC v. accessiBe:** complaint and proposed order announced **January 2025**, final order approved **April 2025** (3-0 vote), **$1 million** payment. Alleged deceptions: (1) claims that accessWidget could make any website WCAG-compliant were false, misleading or unsubstantiated; (2) third-party articles and reviews were formatted to look independent without disclosing material connections. The order bars claims that automated products can make a site WCAG-compliant **or keep it compliant over time** without competent and reliable evidence, and bars misrepresenting endorsers as independent. [V] — [FTC press release, Apr 2025](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million). A 20-year order term is reported by a competitor blog but unverified. [S] — [TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuits-data)
- FTC Act §5 applies to any vendor's marketing claims, including services (FTC substantiation doctrine). [BK] — [FTC Policy Statement on Deception](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-deception)
- Vendor guidance consistently tells merchants to "engage a licensed attorney before responding to any demand letter or complaint". [S] — [TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-website-lawsuit-cost)

### Inferences (practical rules; not legal advice, confirm with a US attorney)
- **Never claim:** "ADA compliant guaranteed", "lawsuit-proof", "100% compliant", "certified ADA compliant" (there is no official certification), "we'll make the lawsuit go away", "protects you from lawsuits", "keeps you compliant forever", or fake or undisclosed testimonials and reviews.
- **Safe phrasing:** "audited against WCAG 2.2 AA by manual and assistive-technology testing", "remediated X issues; conformance report dated ___", "monitoring to detect regressions", "reduces accessibility barriers commonly cited in ADA demand letters", "not legal advice; we are not a law firm".
- **Unauthorized practice of law (UPL):** A non-lawyer, especially a foreign one, must not draft legal responses, assess liability, negotiate settlements or advise on whether to settle. He can deliver a technical audit and remediation plan for the client's attorney, and set up referral or partner relationships with US ADA defense firms. Referral fees to lawyers are generally barred by ABA Model Rule 7.2 [BK], so arrangements should be non-monetary or flat-fee technical subcontracting.
- Keep remediation reports factual and dated, because they may become evidence.

## 6. Professional liability (E&O) insurance and contract clauses

### Takeaway
Little accessibility-specific guidance exists. General professional-services practice applies: carry tech/professional E&O (covers defense if a client claims faulty work, for example after being sued), and use contracts with scope limits, no-guarantee language, a liability cap (commonly fees paid) and client responsibility for third-party code and platform-controlled parts such as Shopify checkout.

### Cited Findings
- Insurers and brokers flag ADA compliance as an E&O exposure for technology professionals and website providers. [S] — [USLI underwriting updates](https://underwritingupdates.usli.com/?p=16311); [IA Magazine, "Why your outdated website may be an E&O risk"](https://www.iamagazine.com/?p=3541)
- In design and architecture work, E&O policies cover defense and sometimes rectification costs. Limitation-of-liability clauses are the standard tool for managing E&O exposure. [S] — [Burns & Wilcox](https://www.burnsandwilcox.com/insights/architect-to-pay-450k-to-settle-lawsuit-over-inaccessible-senior-housing/); [JD Supra Design Professionals](https://www.jdsupra.com/authors/james-wright1/design-professionals/)
- ADA work carries risks that standard contracts may not address, and insurance differs from warranties of compliance. [S] — [Genie AI](https://www.genieai.co/blog/negotiating-insurance-and-warranty-terms-with-ada-compliant-contractors)
- One example: an architect paid **$450k** to settle a suit over an inaccessible building. This is physical ADA, but it shows that vendors themselves can be pulled into claims. [S] — [Burns & Wilcox](https://www.burnsandwilcox.com/insights/architect-to-pay-450k-to-settle-lawsuit-over-inaccessible-senior-housing/)

### Inferences
- Recommended clauses: (1) scope (pages, templates and user flows tested, the WCAG version, assistive technologies and browsers used); (2) **no guarantee** of legal compliance or of freedom from claims; (3) a point-in-time conformance statement, since content changes break it; (4) exclusions for third-party apps, platform-controlled checkout and client edits; (5) **limitation of liability capped at fees paid** (12 months); (6) disclaimers that this is not legal advice and the vendor is not a law firm; (7) client indemnity for content it controls; (8) governing law and venue. Some US clients may require a US entity (for example, an LLC) and a certificate of insurance.
- A Brazilian provider should check whether a US-admitted E&O policy (tech E&O / cyber) is available to a foreign-owned LLC. Premiums were not found.

### Gaps

## 7. Market size: US stores and accessibility spending

### Takeaway
There are millions of US online stores: about 3.7M US sites detected as Shopify by BuiltWith (an inflated measure), about 2.8M live Shopify stores worldwide by StoreLeads' stricter count, and about 4.2-4.3M live WooCommerce stores worldwide (no US split found). There are about 13-14M e-commerce sites in the US overall. With about 4,000-6,000 suits a year, the annual chance of a given small store being sued is low (well under 1%), but the cost per incident is high. Fear, more than probability, drives purchases.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify in the US: about **3,748,162** sites (BuiltWith code detection; overcounts). [S] — [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/how-many-shopify-stores-are-there/); [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/number-of-shopify-stores)
- Shopify globally: about **2.83M live stores** (StoreLeads, Feb 2026). [S] — [Elogic](https://elogic.co/?p=475492)
- WooCommerce globally: **about 4.15-4.34M live stores** (StoreLeads 2026). No US split found. [S] — [Elogic](https://elogic.co/?p=475492)
- US e-commerce sites: **about 13-14M** (estimate). [S] — [Elogic](https://elogic.co/?p=475492)

### Inferences
- Lawsuits run at about 5,000-6,000 a year, while revenue-generating US stores number in the low millions. The base rate per store is about 0.1-0.5% a year (a rough estimate), concentrated in stores with real traffic and revenue (about $1M-$50M). Prospect the stores with meaningful revenue, not hobby shops.
- Sales triggers to watch for: recently sued peers in the same niche, a received demand letter (refer to an attorney first), entry into the EU (European Accessibility Act, in force since June 2025 [BK]) and B2B or government buyers asking for a VPAT or ACR.

### Gaps
- No verified figure for US spending on digital accessibility services or market growth was found. Analyst reports (for example, digital accessibility software market sizing) were not retrieved.
- No US-only WooCommerce or BigCommerce counts were found.
