# Competitive Landscape & Pricing: ADA/WCAG Accessibility Services for US SMB E-commerce (2026)

Research date: 2026-10-25. Method: web search plus page fetches. Several primary vendor sites (accessible.org, allaccessible.org, accessibility.works, adacompliancepros.com) could not be fetched directly (DNS failure from the research environment), so their figures come from search-result excerpts of those pages. Many "pricing guides" are written by vendors who sell competing services (TestParty, Silktide, AudioEye, accessible.org, ADA Compliance Pros). Treat their numbers as asking prices or marketing, not neutral benchmarks.

Labels used below:
- **[LIST]**: a published list price (vendor page or a consistent directory listing).
- **[THIRD-PARTY]**: from a directory or review site such as Capterra, G2 or GetApp; may be stale.
- **[EST]**: an estimate or range from a guide or blog.
- **[QUOTE-ONLY]**: no public price.

## 1. Categories, named players and prices

### Takeaway
Overlay and widget vendors sell cheap annual subscriptions, about $490–$4,000 per year tiered by traffic or pages. Most audit and remediation agencies serving SMBs, and every enterprise firm, are quote-only. The few published agency prices cluster at $750–$2,750 for an audit-only engagement. Remediation is almost never fixed-price. Freelancers sit at the bottom ($50–$120 Upwork fix gigs). The middle ground, a fixed-price manual audit plus code fixes for a Shopify or WooCommerce store, has almost no published offers.

### Inferences
- The market has a price cliff. The cheap options are a widget at about $40–$330/month equivalent, or a freelancer at $50–$120 doing automated-scan fixes. The next step up is about $1,250+ for an audit-only engagement, with remediation unpriced on top (implied at 1–3x the audit, so a realistic all-in of about $2.5k–$8k for a small store).
- Enterprise players (Level Access, Allyant, BOIA, 216digital) hide prices. A transparent fixed price is a real differentiator by itself.

## 2. Packaging, guarantees and fine print

### Takeaway
Overlays package by traffic or page volume and attach "litigation support" or guarantees that are eligibility-gated and limited to "valid claims" on certified pages. Agencies package audits by page or template sample, and sell remediation, VPAT/ACR and monitoring as separate line items. Bundled "audit + fix + retest + statement + demand-letter support" at a fixed price is rare publicly.

### Cited Findings
- Overlay packaging uses tiers by visits (accessiBe, UserWay) or scanned pages (EqualWeb). Audits, VPATs and PDF work are sold through separate services arms. — [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/accessibe/pricing); [Capterra EqualWeb](https://www.capterra.com/p/233808/Equalweb/)
- The AudioEye guarantee is limited to certified pages, valid claims, and the Managed tier with Assurance, "subject to the terms of an applicable Order". — [audioeye.com](https://audioeye.com/solution/audioeye-assurance)
- accessiBe litigation support is "$15k+, terms and conditions apply" and depends on plan eligibility. — [accessibilitychecker.org](https://www.accessibilitychecker.org/guides/accessibe-review)
- Agency audits use a representative sample, not all pages. — [accessible.org/pricing](https://accessible.org/pricing/)
- The ACR/VPAT is sold as a separate document ($350–$950) after the audit. — [accessible.org/cost-vpat](https://accessible.org/cost-vpat/)
- Remediation is scoped separately after the audit. — [abunchofcreators.com](https://www.abunchofcreators.com/services/web-accessibility)
- Monitoring is priced by risk profile (216digital) rather than as a published plan. — [216digital](https://216digital.com/do-you-need-web-accessibility-monitoring/)
- Turnaround claims: TestParty 2 weeks for Shopify theme remediation; Upwork gigs 1–3 days for scan-level fixes. — [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/product/shopify-accessibility); [upwork](https://www.upwork.com/services/product/development-it-fix-shopify-accessibility-issues-wcag-ada-compliance-expert-1777731110640226304)
- Regulatory constraint on claims: the FTC's final order (April 2025, 3-0 vote) requires accessiBe to pay $1M. It bars claims that its tool makes any site WCAG-compliant or ensures ongoing compliance without evidence, and addresses undisclosed material connections in reviews. It is a 20-year consent order. — [FTC press release](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million)

### Inferences
- A new entrant must not promise "100% ADA compliant" or "lawsuit-proof". The FTC order is the precedent. Promise deliverables (WCAG 2.2 AA conformance on a defined set of templates, verified by manual testing, with documentation) instead of outcomes.
- An honest, narrow, well-defined guarantee beats an overlay's vague pledge. Examples: "if a demand letter cites an issue on a template we certified, we fix it free within 5 business days", or a money-back guarantee if the audit isn't delivered on time.
- Legal support should be a referral to partner ADA defense attorneys, not legal advice. Unauthorized practice of law is a risk.

### Gaps
- No public fine print found for UserWay's guarantee beyond the competitor's characterization.
- No public data on how often overlay guarantees actually pay out.

## 3. Customer complaints and objections (the gap)

### Takeaway
Buyers complain that overlays don't prevent lawsuits (about 22–28% of 2025 digital accessibility suits targeted sites with widgets), that billing and cancellation are hard, and that audits deliver a report but no fixes. Settlements require source-code remediation, not widgets.

### Cited Findings
- UsableNet 2025 year-end (via secondary sources): sites with overlays were named in suits more than 1,400 times (1,416), at 95–155 new cases per month; 1,023 in 2024. — [silktide.com](https://silktide.com/s/overlays-got-1416-sites-sued-in-2025/); [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuits-data)
- Overlay share of suits: 28% in 2025, up from 25% in 2024, per a UsableNet summary. A CourtListener review of H1 2025 found 456 cases (22.6%). The sources conflict. — [andava.com](https://www.andava.com/learn/web-accessibility-statistics/); [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuits-data)
- Lawsuit volume: more than 5,000 ADA website suits in 2025 (UsableNet via Fox Rothschild). Seyfarth counts 3,117 federal filings, up 27%. The scopes differ. — [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/events/ada-website-lawsuit-trends-what-2025-filings-mean-for-2026); [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuits-data)
- E-commerce makes up about 70% of cases [EZUD; unverified primary]. 30% of the top 500 e-commerce retailers were sued in 2024. — [ezud.com](https://ezud.com/worst-accessibility-fails-lessons-learned/); [QualityLogic](https://www.qualitylogic.com/knowledge-center/accessibility-industry-update-january-2025/)
- "38.5% of sued organizations thought they were already covered" [Silktide, a competitor; unverified]. — [silktide.com](https://silktide.com/s/overlays-got-1416-sites-sued-in-2025/)
- The NFB's 2021 resolution says overlay providers "make misleading, unproven, and unethical claims". Settlements "almost universally require source code remediation". — [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/blog/shopify-accessibility-widgets-the-compliance-illusion-merchants-buy)
- Settlement cost estimates (vendor sources, all [EST]): demand letters settle for $1,500–$5,000 with a prompt good-faith response; other estimates put average settlements at $5k–$20k and formal litigation at $25k–$75k. Respond to demand letters within 30–60 days and to federal complaints within 21 days. — [testparty.ai demand letter guide](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-demand-letter-shopify-merchant-guide); [testparty.ai playbook](https://testparty.ai/blog/ecommerce-ada-lawsuit-playbook); [ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/shopify-ada-lawsuit-trap/)
- Billing and cancellation complaints about accessiBe:
  - On Trustpilot, a reseller says a 30-day cancellation rule caused 6 client accounts to auto-renew at about $600 each. — [Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/accessibe.com)
  - On the Shopify App Store, a merchant says they were charged after deactivating and were bounced between Shopify and accessiBe. — [apps.shopify.com](https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/2361668)
  - Another review reports "billed an extra $900" and difficulty cancelling. — [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/accessibe/pricing)
- UserWay Shopify reviews: the paid plan didn't improve compliance, and a merchant was sued while subscribed; the vendor replied by pointing to its legal support. — [appnavigator.io](https://appnavigator.io/app/userway-accessibility/reviews/)
- Merchants remain responsible for theme customizations and third-party scripts, and a single plugin can introduce failures. — [a11y-collective](https://www.a11y-collective.com/blog/shopify-ada-compliance/); [consentmo](https://www.consentmo.com/blog-posts/accessibility-lawsuits-and-shopify-stores-what-every-merchant-should-know-in-2025)
- Settling one suit doesn't prevent suits from other plaintiffs. — [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/blog/you-can-be-fully-compliant-and-still-get-sued-inside-the-ada-demand-letter-econom)

### Inferences
- The objections that define the gap:
  - "I paid for a widget and still got sued."
  - "I paid $2k+ for a PDF report and no one fixed anything."
  - "I can't get a price without a sales call."
  - "Auto-renewal and cancellation traps."
  - "Third-party apps keep breaking it."
- The winning message: real code fixes in your theme, a fixed price shown on the page, and no lock-in.

### Gaps
- Reddit threads (r/shopify, r/accessibility) did not surface in searches, and the Reddit MCP was unavailable. No direct verbatim Reddit quotes were collected.
- G2 review text was not retrieved.

## 4. Underserved segment and proposed tiers

### Takeaway
Shopify and WooCommerce stores with roughly $500k–$10M in revenue are underserved. They are too big to rely on a $490 widget, which is exactly what plaintiffs target, and too small for quote-only enterprise firms. No visible competitor publishes a fixed price for audit plus in-theme fixes plus retest. A three-tier offer can be priced between Upwork ($50–$120) and agencies (about $1,250 audit-only, with remediation on top).

### Cited Findings (anchors for pricing)
- Audit-only anchors:
  - accessible.org: $1,250–$2,750 typical; $1,250–$2,250 for a simple Shopify store. — [accessible.org](https://accessible.org/pricing/)
  - Other firms: $750 for 5 pages, $1,500 for 10 pages. thefrontkit: $499 (≤5 pages) and $2,400 (≤12 pages). — [thefrontkit](https://thefrontkit.com/blogs/accessibility-audit-cost-2026)
- Remediation adds 1–3x the audit fee. — [digitala11y](https://digitala11y.com/how-much-does-a-web-accessibility-audit-cost)
- Widget anchors: accessiBe $490–$3,990/yr; EqualWeb $590–$2,990/yr; AudioEye from $49/month. — sources above.
- ACR anchor: $350 (WCAG edition). — [accessible.org/cost-vpat](https://accessible.org/cost-vpat/)
- Demand-letter settlement anchor: $1,500–$5,000 with a prompt good-faith response [EST]. — [testparty.ai](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-demand-letter-shopify-merchant-guide)

### Inferences: proposed 3-tier offer (these are recommendations, not market data)
An e-commerce store typically has about 6–8 templates: home, collection/category, product, cart, checkout (Shopify checkout is largely platform-controlled), search, account/login, and a content page.

1. **"Standard Fix" (core offer): $2,490 fixed, 10 business days**
   - Manual and automated WCAG 2.2 AA audit of up to 8 templates, keyboard and screen-reader testing (NVDA/VoiceOver), in-theme code fixes, one retest round, an accessibility statement, and a conformance summary.
   - Competitor equivalent: accessible.org audit-only at $1,250–$2,750, plus remediation at 1–3x quoted separately, for a likely all-in of $3k–$8k.
   - Positioning: "about the price of their audit alone, but fixed."
   - Optional upsells: extra templates at $150–$250 each (in line with the $100–$250 per-page market rate); a WCAG-edition ACR/VPAT at $350–$500.

2. **"Demand Letter Rapid Response": $3,990 fixed, triage within 48h, fixes within 5–7 business days**
   - Everything in Standard, plus priority: fix the issues cited in the letter first, a dated remediation log and evidence pack for counsel, and a referral to partner ADA defense attorneys (no legal advice).
   - Competitor equivalent: no visible published SMB product. Alternatives are overlay "litigation support" (eligibility-gated, $15k+ pledge with T&Cs, or AudioEye Assurance on the Managed tier only) or enterprise quotes.
   - Justification: cheaper than a typical settlement range ($5k–$20k [EST]), and documented good-faith remediation supports a lower settlement ($1.5k–$5k [EST]).

3. **"Monitor & Maintain": $149/month (≤10k SKUs; annual $1,490), or $299/month with 2 hours of fixes included**
   - Monthly automated scans plus a manual spot-check of new templates and apps, quarterly retests, statement updates, and priority fixes when theme or app updates break things. Month-to-month with no auto-renew trap.
   - Competitor equivalent: accessiBe Growth $1,490/yr or AudioEye from $49/month, which are automation-only; 216digital a11y.Radar is quote-only.
   - Position it explicitly as "human monitoring, not an overlay." It directly answers the billing and cancellation complaints.

Guarantee ideas that stay FTC-safe:
- A money-back guarantee if the deadline is missed.
- A "Fix-it-free" warranty: any WCAG 2.2 AA failure on a certified template found within 12 months, including issues cited in a demand letter, is fixed at no charge within 5 business days.
- No claims of "lawsuit-proof" or "100% compliant".

Delivery economics for remote/outsourced work (inference): roughly 15–30 hours for an 8-template audit plus fixes on a standard Shopify Online Store 2.0 theme. At offshore developer rates this supports healthy margins at $2,490. This needs validation; no source was found.

### Gaps
- Revenue-band data (whether $500k–$10M stores are the most-sued) was not found. The e-commerce share of suits (about 70%) and the top-500 retailer data are the only proxies.
- No source gave actual hours per template for remediation. The hours estimate is my own inference.
- No direct data on buyer willingness to pay. The tiers are triangulated from competitor anchors only.
