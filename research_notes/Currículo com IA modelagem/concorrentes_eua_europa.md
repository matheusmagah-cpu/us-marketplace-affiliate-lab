# Teardown of resume/CV-builder competitors: US, UK, DE, FR, ES, MX (as of Oct 2026)

Labels used: **[V]** = verified from a primary source (vendor page, statute, or law-firm analysis citing primary documents). **[S]** = secondary source (review or affiliate blog, often written by a competitor). **[E]** = estimate or inference by the researcher. **[K]** = from the researcher's background knowledge and not re-verified this session (check before relying on it).
Method caveat: most pricing pages block automated fetching, and several domains (micvideal.mx, rocket-resume.com, hrexecutive.com) could not be reached from this sandbox. Most prices therefore come from 2026 review blogs that quote vendor pages, and many of those blogs are run by competing builders.

## 1. Leaders and market structure

### Takeaway
The paid-search resume-builder market in the US, UK and the Latin-language markets is dominated by BOLD's network of look-alike brands (Zety, LiveCareer, MyPerfectResume/MyPerfectCV, ResumeNow, ResumeHelp, Moncvparfait, Micvideal and others). A private antitrust suit filed in April 2026 alleges that BOLD controls more than 20 brands and over 80% of the online resume market. The second cluster is Resume.io and its sister local brands (e.g. CVapp.mx), which use a near-identical trial-to-subscription model. Germany stands apart: its local leader, Lebenslauf.de, sells fixed-term passes that end by themselves.

## 2. Funnel step by step for the top players (BOLD / Resume.io pattern)

### Takeaway
The dominant funnel works like this. A "Free resume builder" hook leads to building without an account. The user sees a full preview, then hits a paywall at download because the free tier allows TXT only. They pay a $1.95–2.95 trial of 7–14 days, which auto-renews at $23.95–29.95 every 4 weeks. Billing every 4 weeks means 13 charges a year. Cancellation friction is the main complaint driver.

### Cited Findings
- Zety: free tier allows .txt download only. Pro trial is $1.95 for 14 days (some regions $2.70–2.95). It auto-renews at $25.95 billed every 4 weeks; the pricing-page text reads "auto-renews at $25.95 billed every 4 weeks. Cancel anytime." Annual plan is $5.95/mo ($71.40 upfront). Four-week billing means 13 charges/yr, about $337/yr. ### Inferences
- [E] Typical funnel to copy or beat:
  1. Ad or landing page ("Free AI resume builder, ready in minutes").
  2. Pick a template.
  3. Guided step-by-step form: contact, experience with AI bullet suggestions, education, skills, summary.
  4. Live preview of the full formatted resume.
  5. Click "Download", then create an account (email) and see the paywall.
  6. Choose a plan: the low-price trial is pre-selected and the annual plan is shown as a per-month saving.
  7. Checkout.
  8. Upsell cover letter and ATS check (usually included in the subscription).
  9. Auto-renew on day 7 or 14.
- [E] The paywall sits after the user has invested effort (sunk cost), which is why conversion works even with TXT-only free tiers.
- [E] Monetization depends heavily on renewal "slippage". A $1.95 trial cannot pay a US CPC of about US$4–9, so these players depend on 1+ renewals per buyer.

### Gaps
- No primary walkthrough of the cancellation flows (number of screens, retention offers) could be captured. Human resume-review upsell pricing was not found.
- Exact Micvideal MX price in MXN not found (domain unreachable).

## 4. Regulation affecting subscription traps (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
- **US**: the FTC click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025, and the FTC restarted rulemaking with an ANPRM in March 2026. Enforcement continues under ROSCA and Section 5. California's amended Automatic Renewal Law (since July 1, 2025) is the binding constraint, and a California ARL suit against BOLD was filed in April 2026.
- **UK**: the DMCC subscription regime is now expected in January 2027 (it had been pushed to spring 2027).
- **EU**: a mandatory "withdraw from contract" button applies from June 19, 2026, on top of existing national cancellation buttons in Germany and France.

### Cited Findings
- The Eighth Circuit vacated the entire FTC Negative Option ("click-to-cancel") Rule in July 2025 (Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC). The ground was procedural: no preliminary regulatory analysis. [V law firm] — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-07-11-click-to-cancel-just-got-cancelled-eighth-circuit-vacates-entirety-of-ftcs-negative-option-rule); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-voided)
- On March 11, 2026, the FTC issued an ANPRM on the Negative Option Rule, with comments due April 13, 2026. ROSCA and Section 5 enforcement continue. [V law firm] — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/); [Jones Day, May 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur)
- California AB 2863 applies to contracts from July 1, 2025. It requires:
  - coverage of free-to-pay conversions (trials);
  - express affirmative consent to the renewal terms;
  - consent records kept for 3 years, or 1 year after termination if longer;
  - annual reminders;
  - cancellation through the same channel as sign-up ("click to cancel");
  - clear notice of price changes. [V law firm] — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect); [Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/california-tightens-requirements-for-automatically-renewing-subscriptions); [KTS](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2024/10/california-latest-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-in-july-2025)
- A suit filed April 9, 2026 in San Diego County alleges BOLD runs "an automatic renewal scheme" against California consumers under the ARL and UCL. Class status not confirmed. [S] — [haired.app](https://www.haired.app/blog/zety-review-2026)
- No FTC action specifically against BOLD or any resume builder was found. [gap/negative finding]
- UK DMCC Act subscription regime: the 2 April 2026 government response pushed commencement from autumn 2026 to spring 2027. On 10 August 2026 the Prime Minister brought it forward to January 2027 (per TLT and Brodies). Guidance is still pending. [V law firm] — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/dmcc-subscription-rules-delayed-to-spring-2027-key-takeaways-from-the-consultation-response/); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/subscription-contracts); [TLT](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/dmcc-act-subscription-contracts-regime-brought-forward-by-the-pm-what-do-businesses-need-to-know); [Limio](https://www.limio.com/guides/dmcca-subscriptions)
- EU Directive 2023/2673 introduces a withdrawal button for distance contracts from 19 June 2026. It must be labelled "withdraw from contract here" or an equivalent, followed by "confirm withdrawal", with an acknowledgement on a durable medium. Germany transposed it as § 356a BGB (law published 5 Feb 2026). [V law firm] — [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/New-EU-Withdrawal-Button-Requirement-Practical-Implications-and-Recommendations-for-US-and-Global-Online-Sellers-7-8-2026); [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/world-consumer-rights-day-part-3-mandatory-withdrawal-button-coming-june-2026/); [kpw.law](https://kpw.law/en/new-withdrawal-button-2026/); [Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/new-eu-rules-on-withdrawing-from-online-contracts/)
- [K] Germany § 312k BGB "Kündigungsbutton" (since July 1, 2022): any online-concluded continuing contract needs a "Verträge hier kündigen" button plus a confirmation page. The Fair Consumer Contracts Act also limits automatic extensions to monthly-cancellable terms. Not re-verified this session.
- [K] France: "résiliation en trois clics" (Loi pouvoir d'achat 2022, in force June 1, 2023) requires an easily accessible online termination function. Loi Chatel requires reminders before tacit renewal. Not re-verified this session.
- [K] EU 14-day withdrawal right for digital content: it is lost only if the consumer expressly consents to immediate performance and acknowledges losing the right. This matters for "download now" one-off sales.

### Inferences
- [E] Trial-to-subscription models carry growing legal risk in CA, the EU and (from 2027) the UK. A one-off or fixed-term pass sidesteps most auto-renewal law and is a credible marketing message ("no subscription, nothing to cancel").
- [E] An entrant selling digital downloads to EU consumers must still implement the withdrawal-waiver checkbox and, from June 2026, the withdrawal button. A merchant-of-record provider may handle part of this.

### Gaps
- No court rulings yet in the BOLD cases. No BBB complaint counts retrieved.

## 6. Weak points for an entrant and feasibility for a Brazil-based, faceless owner

### Takeaway
The clearest gaps are:
- billing distrust of trial-trap brands;
- TXT-only "free" tiers that feel like bait-and-switch;
- overpriced local offers in MX, ES and FR (about €25 or MXN 499 per 4 weeks);
- rising regulatory pressure.

A merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy or Stripe Managed Payments) removes the VAT and sales-tax burden. Brazil-specific payout terms need direct confirmation.
